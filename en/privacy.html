Privacy Policy

OneMouse connects Windows with Android, other Windows PCs, and Macs. This policy covers existing LAN/Bluetooth pairing and optional Google account linking, including data handling and permissions.

Effective date: September 1, 2026 · Last updated: October 31, 2026

Google account data · Request account and data deletion

01

Core principle

Existing LAN/Bluetooth pairing works without sign-in. In supported app versions, optional Google account linking finds your own account devices and attempts direct connections across networks. The OneMouse account server processes connection metadata and signals; it does not store or relay files, clipboard content, input, or video.

02

Information processed

  • Device information: Android device ID, device name, Windows or Mac computer name, device ID, platform, display information, or local network address
  • Connection information: pairing state, session key, selected device, transport, and last connection time
  • Placement information: screen size, orientation, display topology, and device position around the Windows PC
  • Transfer information: files, folders, text, and clipboard content explicitly sent by the user
  • Billing information: whether Android Pro was purchased or restored. Google Play handles payment processing.
  • Notification information (optional): if you turn on "Send notifications to PC" in the Android app and allow notification access, the title and text of notifications from the apps you select are sent to your paired PC and shown briefly. The Windows PC can keep an encrypted local history of notification titles and text, app and device details, receipt times, and whether an image was present. This history is not uploaded to a server.

03

Accessibility and screen permissions

Android Accessibility applies mouse and keyboard input sent from the PC to Android. On Mac, Accessibility allows the Mac to perform input received from Windows. Local Network is used for discovery on macOS 15+ and may not appear on macOS 13/14. Mac Bluetooth is outside the current public support scope. Screen & System Audio Recording is optional and used only for LAN-based Mac display sharing; OneMouse does not capture system audio. These permissions are not used for ad tracking, password collection, or remote server transmission.

04

Files and clipboard

Files, folders, and clipboard content move only when the user sends them or enables the relevant sync feature. Transfers are direct among supported devices, including Mac over LAN. Content is not uploaded to OneMouse servers.

05

Local storage and deletion

Pairing records, session keys, device lists, and placement settings may be stored in each app's local storage. The Mac app stores its paired session key in the macOS Keychain. Clearing pairing removes the trusted session record; users can separately remove app data and settings from their devices.

Notification history is enabled by default and keeps only newly received notifications on the PC for 7 days, up to 200 entries. It does not import older notifications from your phone. In the PC's Notifications tab, you can choose 1, 7, or 30 days, stop saving new entries, or clear all history.

Notifications detected as containing verification codes and notifications with hidden or redacted content are excluded. Clipboard content, photos, and avatar images are not saved in history. Not all sensitive content can be detected automatically. History is not saved if secure operating-system encryption is unavailable.

Turning off history does not delete existing entries; they remain until they expire or you delete them. Entries that expire while the app is closed are cleaned up at the next launch. Clearing PC history does not delete notifications on your phone.

06

Ads and billing

Google Play Billing, when applicable, is limited to the Android distribution, and no ads SDK is included in any app. The Windows and macOS apps do not process purchases or transfer Android Pro entitlement between platforms. Google's own terms and privacy policies may apply to Android billing services.

07

Website analytics

This website uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to understand visit statistics. It sets no cookies or browser storage and creates no identifier for individual visitors; it processes only aggregate statistics such as the pages viewed, the referrer, the country, and the browser and device type. It does not collect directly identifying information such as your name or contact details.

The download button on the home page queries the GitHub releases list so it can point to the latest version. Your IP address and browser information are sent to GitHub as part of that request.

This applies to the website only — the OneMouse apps contain no analytics SDK.

08

App update checks

Each app periodically downloads a version information file so it can tell you about new versions and about compatibility between your devices. The Android app checks version information on onemouse.pages.dev; the Windows and macOS apps check GitHub release information.

These requests contain no device identifiers, pairing information, account information, or usage statistics — the apps only download the file. As with any internet request, your IP address and the time of the request may appear in server logs. Installer downloads happen only when you explicitly choose to download.

09

Optional Google account linking

We use the Google-verified account identifier (sub) and verified email address, when provided, to identify and display your account. We do not receive your Google password or access your contacts, email messages, or Google Drive files.

The account service stores registered devices' public keys, public-key-based IDs, names and types, optional LAN device IDs, PC roles (main/sub), registration and last-seen times, session expiry, and connection state. These support same-account device discovery, connection validation, and security. Device private keys and Google access/ID tokens are not stored on the server; app session tokens are stored there as hashes.

Google verifies sign-in identity. Cloudflare Workers and SQLite Durable Objects store account/device metadata and forward connection signals. SDP/ICE signals may include IP addresses and other network information; they are forwarded to the other device but not stored in the OneMouse account database. Providers such as Google and Cloudflare may process IP addresses and other network metadata when serving requests.

Account and device registration records may remain until the device is unregistered or the OneMouse account is deleted. App sessions last up to 7 days, temporary sign-in flows up to 10 minutes, and one-use connection tickets 30 seconds. Expired temporary records are eligible for periodic server cleanup; replay-prevention and rate-limit records also expire. Closing, disconnecting, or uninstalling the app does not itself delete server registrations.

Signing in alone does not start screen sharing. Apps validate the current device, session, and signature for same-account connection requests. Direct connections can fail on some networks.

10

Request OneMouse account and data deletion

In the Android app, use Google account → Account management → Delete account link to delete your server-side OneMouse account, registered devices, and sessions. If reauthentication is required, sign out, sign in again, and retry within five minutes. This does not delete your Google account or existing LAN/Bluetooth pairings.

If you cannot access the app or have uninstalled it, email sdlckdrl@gmail.com to request account deletion. Identify the Google email address used with OneMouse and state that you request account deletion. Account ownership verification may be needed. Do not send your Google password or sign-in tokens.

Signing out unregisters the current device and its account sessions; it is different from deleting the entire OneMouse account. If the server cannot be reached, only local sign-out may complete and the server registration can remain. Delete transferred files and local notification history separately on each device.
